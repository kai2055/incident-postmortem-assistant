## Summary

From October 28 to October 31, 2021, Roblox experienced a 73-hour outage that completely took down the platform. Fifty million daily active users were unable to access Roblox during this period. The outage was caused by two distinct but related performance issues in Consul, the service discovery system used by Roblox to manage its infrastructure. A new streaming feature in Consul, enabled months earlier, created excessive contention (blocking) under high read and write load. This contention was exacerbated by a pathological performance issue in BoltDB, the open-source database used by Consul to store its logs, where a 7.8MB freelist had to be written for every 16KB log write. The combination of issues caused the Consul cluster to become unhealthy, making it impossible for Roblox services to find each other and communicate. The outage was extended by circular dependencies in monitoring systems that made diagnosing the problem difficult, and by the time required to bootstrap services from a fully-down state. There was no user data loss or unauthorized access.

## Timeline

- **Months before** — Consul streaming feature enabled on a subset of Roblox services to reduce CPU and network usage
- **October 27, 14:00 PST** — Streaming feature enabled on a backend service responsible for traffic routing. Node count increased by 50% in preparation for end-of-year traffic
- **October 28, ~13:37 PST** — Vault performance degraded. Single Consul server has high CPU load. Engineers begin investigation. Players not yet impacted
- **October 28, 16:35 PST** — Players drop to 50% of normal. System health significantly degrades. Complete outage begins
- **October 28, ~19:00 PST** — Consul cluster migrated to new, more powerful machines (128 cores, NVME SSDs). Cluster remains unhealthy
- **October 29, 02:00–04:00 PST** — First return-to-service attempt: Consul cluster shut down and restored from snapshot. Fails
- **October 29, 04:00 – October 30, 02:00 PST** — Second attempt: Consul usage reduced across all services. Fails
- **October 30, 02:00–12:00 PST** — Team investigates Consul internals. Discovers contention in streaming code paths. Switches from 128-core back to 64-core servers. No improvement
- **October 30, 12:00–20:00 PST** — Root causes identified: streaming feature causing contention; BoltDB freelist performance issue
- **October 30, ~15:51 PST** — Streaming feature disabled. 50th percentile KV write latency drops from 2 seconds to 300ms. Breakthrough
- **October 30, 20:00 – October 31, 05:00 PST** — Caching system redeployed
- **October 31, 05:00 PST** — Consul and caching system healthy. Ready to bring up services
- **October 31, ~10:00 PST** — Services restarted. Ready to open to players
- **October 31, 10:00–16:45 PST** — DNS steering used to gradually allow players back in ~10% increments
- **October 31, 16:45 PST** — 100% of players allowed access. Roblox fully operational

## Root Cause

The outage was caused by two distinct issues:

1. **Consul streaming feature contention** — A new streaming feature in Consul, designed to reduce CPU and network usage, was enabled on a subset of services months before the outage. On October 27, the feature was enabled on a traffic routing service with a 50% node increase. Under very high read and write load, the streaming implementation caused excessive contention (blocking) on a single Go channel. This made write operations significantly slower. The issue was worsened by 128-core servers (dual-socket NUMA architecture) which increased contention on shared resources.

2. **BoltDB freelist performance issue** — Consul uses BoltDB (a key-value store) to store its Raft logs (write-ahead logs for leader election and data replication). BoltDB never shrinks its disk space; it marks deleted data as "free" and reuses it. Under Roblox's workload, the freelist (which tracks free pages) grew to nearly 1 million entries (7.8MB). Every 16KB log write required writing the entire 7.8MB freelist to disk, causing 2-3 second write latency.

Additional factors:

- **Single cluster failure domain** — All Roblox services relied on a single Consul cluster. When Consul became unhealthy, all services were affected
- **Monitoring circular dependency** — Critical monitoring systems depended on Consul. When Consul was unhealthy, the team lacked visibility into the problem
- **Slow bootstrapping** — Bringing Roblox up from a fully-down state required redeploying the caching system, which took 9 hours due to tooling designed for incremental changes, not full bootstrapping

---

## Resolution

1. **Root cause identification** — After 2 days of investigation, engineers identified the streaming feature as the primary cause. Disabled streaming at 15:51 on October 30. KV write latency dropped to normal levels.

2. **Workaround for slow leaders** — Some Consul leaders still exhibited slow performance due to BoltDB freelist issues. The team prevented problematic leaders from staying elected, allowing the cluster to remain stable.

3. **Caching system redeployment** — The caching system (1B+ requests/sec) was unhealthy and had to be redeployed from scratch. This took 9 hours due to incorrect scheduling data and deployment tool limitations. Engineers worked through the night to resolve issues.

4. **Service restart** — Running services were restarted at correct capacity levels and verified.

5. **Gradual player re-entry** — DNS steering was used to gradually allow players back in ~10% increments. This allowed the team to monitor system stability (database load, cache performance) after each increase.

---

## Prevention

- **Telemetry independence** — Removed circular dependency between monitoring systems and Consul. Telemetry systems no longer depend on the systems they monitor. Added better visibility into Consul and BoltDB performance with targeted alerts.

- **Infrastructure resilience** — Building out additional, geographically distinct data centers. Moving to multiple availability zones. Sharding Consul clusters: splitting critical services into their own dedicated clusters. Migrating KV data to more appropriate storage systems.

- **Consul improvements (with HashiCorp)** — HashiCorp creating new benchmarks to reproduce the contention issue and improving streaming design. Replacing BoltDB with bbolt (successor) that does not have the unbounded freelist growth issue. Upgrade completed in Q1 2022.

- **Bootstrapping improvements** — New tools and processes to make cache deployment from a standing start faster and more automated. Nomad enhancements to turn up large jobs after long periods of unavailability.

- **Streaming re-introduction** — Once a new implementation is tested at Roblox's scale, streaming will be carefully reintroduced.
